Beyond the Screen

One flooded street, one buzzing phone, and the moment a boy steps to the other side of the glass.

$ step beyond the screen
21:47Indoors

At 9:47 p.m., the group chat of Barangay San Isidro had 212 unread messages.

Miko scrolled through them from his bed, his thumb moving on its own.

San Isidro Neighbors · 212 unread
Aling CoraGrabe, bahang-baha na naman sa kanto!Terrible, the corner is flooded again!
Kuya DindoWala talagang ginagawa ang barangay.The barangay really does nothing.
Mica R.Sino ba ang maglilinis niyan?Who is even going to clean that up?

Under the messages sat a photo of the clogged canal at the corner, its black water creeping across the road. Four hundred reactions. Not one person, as far as Miko could tell, had actually gone outside.

He typed his own comment, true, sobrang hassle, and watched it collect hearts. For a second, it felt like helping.

22:03The window

Outside, something scraped against concrete.

He pulled back the curtain. Under the streetlight, Aling Rosa, who sold fish balls at the corner and had to be at least seventy, knelt at the canal, pulling plastic bags out of the water with her bare hands. Her cart stood on the dry side of the road, its small bulb flickering.

His phone buzzed.

Aling CoraBakit ba walang tumutulong?Why is nobody helping?

Miko read it twice. In the dark glass he saw his own face, lit blue, and behind it the real street, wet and waiting. He had been standing on the wrong side of the window.

He put the phone face down on the pillow.

22:20Outside

The air smelled of rain and rust.

“Aling Rosa, ako na po,” he said, kneeling beside her.

Let me do it, please.

She squinted up at him. “Ikaw pala, iho. Akala ko puro cellphone ka lang.”

Oh, it’s you, child. I thought you were only about your phone.

It stung because it was fair. He laughed anyway and reached for the next bag.

The water was cold and the plastic was slimy; his sleeves were soaked within a minute. Ten minutes later, a door opened across the street. Kuya Dindo came down in his slippers with a rake. Then two kids with a dustpan, then Ate Joan in gloves three sizes too big. Nobody mentioned the group chat. They only called out to one another: “Dito pa! May barado pa rito!”

Over here! There’s more stuck over here!

23:00After

By eleven, the canal gurgled and the water slid away, taking the whole street’s complaints with it. They stood in a muddy circle, out of breath, laughing at nothing. Aling Rosa handed out fish balls for free. Miko ate his standing up, steam in his face, and realized he now knew the names of four neighbors he had only known as profile pictures.

Upstairs, his phone lit the dark room: 340 unread. Someone had finally written, Sino nag-ayos? Salamat po sa kung sino man.

Who fixed it? Thank you, whoever you are.

Miko didn’t reply. He sat on the curb, hands smelling of rain and canal, watching the street shine under the light. Across the road, Kuya Dindo laughed out loud, the first sound all night that hadn’t come through a speaker.
