Part II · Poem poem.txt
The screen glows like a small, cold moon,
and we orbit it from dawn till noon.
A thousand friends, yet one lone chair,
a crowded world with no one there.
But look: a mother's voice through wire,
a student's hope, a spark, a fire.
A stranger's kind words, typed in light,
can carry someone through the night.
The code can count, the code can learn,
but cannot feel the heart's slow turn;
it cannot hold a trembling hand
or truly, softly, understand.
So let the heart outshine the glow,
and let compassion be what we show.
The future hums in lines of code,
yet love still lights the human road.

Part IV · Reflection reflection.log

> what did writing this poem teach me?

Reflection

I was inspired to write this poem because I noticed how much of my day is spent on my phone, even when my family is right beside me. I wanted to show that technology can connect us, but it can also make us feel lonely if we forget to be present for one another. The message I want readers to remember is that no matter how advanced technology becomes, kindness and compassion are what keep us human. Writing poetry helped me put into words feelings I never really said out loud. Using images like the “cold moon” and the “trembling hand” let me show my emotions instead of just explaining them. In the end, I learned that poetry gives me a voice, and I hope mine reminds people to use technology with a caring heart.
